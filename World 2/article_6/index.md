:::title
Writing Equations
:::

There are several ways to write the equation of a line, depending on what information you're given.

:::mini-title
Point-Slope Form
:::

**Point-slope form** is used when you know the slope of a line and one point on it:

y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)

Here, m is the slope, and (x₁, y₁) is the known point.

:::example
Slope = 3, point (2, 5)
y − 5 = 3(x − 2)
:::

This can be rewritten in slope-intercept form if needed.

:::example
y − 5 = 3(x − 2)
y − 5 = 3x − 6
y = 3x − 1
:::

:::tip
Point-slope form is fastest when you already have a slope and a point—no need to solve for b separately.
:::

:::mini-title
Standard Form
:::

**Standard form** writes a linear equation as:

Ax + By = C

where A, B, and C are integers, and A is usually written as positive.

:::example
Convert y = 2x + 5 to standard form.
y − 2x = 5
−2x + y = 5
2x − y = −5
:::

:::mini-title
Writing from a Graph
:::

To write an equation from a graph, identify the y-intercept and the slope directly from the line, then plug both into slope-intercept form.

:::example
A line crosses the y-axis at (0, 4) and passes through (2, 8).
Slope = (8 − 4)/(2 − 0) = 4/2 = 2
Equation: y = 2x + 4
:::

:::mini-title
Writing from a Table
:::

To write an equation from a table of values, find the slope using any two points, then use one point to solve for b.

:::example
| x | y |  

| 1 | 5 |  

| 3 | 9 |

Slope = (9 − 5)/(3 − 1) = 4/2 = 2

Use (1, 5): 5 = 2(1) + b
b = 3

Equation: y = 2x + 3
:::

:::mini-title
Word Models
:::

Many SAT problems describe a linear relationship in words instead of giving points or a graph directly. The key is identifying the starting value (b) and the rate of change (m).

:::example
A gym charges a $30 sign-up fee plus $15 per month.
Starting value (b) = 30
Rate of change (m) = 15
Equation: y = 15x + 30, where x is the number of months and y is the total cost.
:::

:::tip
Look for a fixed starting amount (b) and a rate that repeats each unit of time (m) when translating a word problem into an equation.
:::
