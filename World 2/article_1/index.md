:::title
One-Variable Equations
:::

An equation is a mathematical statement that two expressions are equal. Solving an equation means finding the value of the variable that makes the statement true.

:::mini-title
One-Step Equations
:::

A one-step equation can be solved using a single operation—addition, subtraction, multiplication, or division. The goal is to isolate the variable by undoing whatever operation is being done to it.

:::example
x + 7 = 12
x = 12 − 7
x = 5
:::

:::example
3x = 21
x = 21 ÷ 3
x = 7
:::

:::tip
Whatever you do to one side of the equation, you must do to the other side.
:::

:::mini-title
Two-Step Equations
:::

A two-step equation requires two operations to solve. Undo addition or subtraction first, then undo multiplication or division.

:::example
2x + 5 = 15
2x = 15 − 5
2x = 10
x = 5
:::

:::

The same steps apply even when the coefficient is negative.

:::example
−4x + 6 = −10
−4x = −10 − 6
−4x = −16
x = 4
:::

:::mini-title
Multi-Step Equations
:::

A multi-step equation requires more than two operations, often including distributing or combining like terms.

If parentheses are present, distribute before combining like terms.

:::example
3(x + 4) = 21
3x + 12 = 21
3x = 21 − 12
3x = 9
x = 3
:::

Combine like terms on each side of the equation before isolating the variable.

:::example
2x + 3x − 5 = 20
5x − 5 = 20
5x = 25
x = 5
:::

Some problems require both steps together.

:::example
4(x − 2) + 3x = 27
4x − 8 + 3x = 27
7x − 8 = 27
7x = 35
x = 5
:::

:::tip
Simplify each side of the equation fully before trying to isolate the variable.
:::

:::mini-title
Variables on Both Sides
:::

Some equations have the variable on both sides of the equal sign. To solve, move all variable terms to one side and all constants to the other.

:::example
5x + 3 = 2x + 12
5x − 2x = 12 − 3
3x = 9
x = 3
:::

:::example
7x − 4 = 3x + 8
7x − 3x = 8 + 4
4x = 12
x = 3
:::

Sometimes combining variable terms results in a special case: no solution or infinitely many solutions.

:::example
2x + 5 = 2x + 9
5 = 9
:::

This statement is false, so this equation has no solution.

:::example
3x + 4 = 3x + 4
4 = 4
:::

This statement is always true, so this equation has infinitely many solutions.

:::tip
If the variable terms cancel out and you're left with a true statement, the equation has infinitely many solutions. If you're left with a false statement, it has no solution.
:::
