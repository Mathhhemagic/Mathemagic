:::title
Ratios and Rates
:::

A ratio is a mathematical comparison that shows the relative size or relationship between two or more quantities, often written using a colon or a fraction.

:::example
There are 5 apples and 4 pears.
The ratio of apples to pears is 5:4 or ⁵⁄₄, which means that for every 5 apples, there are 4 pears.
The ratio of pears to apples is 4:5 or ⅘, which means that for every 4 pears, there are 5 apples.
:::

There are two types of ratios:

**Part-to-part ratios** compare two parts, such as oranges and sugar in orange juice.

**Part-to-whole ratios** compare a part to the whole, such as oranges to the total amount of juice.

:::mini-title
Equivalent Ratios
:::

**Equivalent ratios** are ratios that, when both sides of the colon or both parts of the fraction are multiplied or divided by the same number, result in another equivalent ratio.

:::example
4:5 = 16:20 (Multiply each side of the ratio by 4.)
:::

We can use this to solve problems.

:::example
If it takes 2 cups of sugar and 1 cup of water to make a batch of cookies, how many cups of sugar would we need if we have 4 cups of water?

2:1 = x:4

Multiply both sides by 4:

x = 8
:::

Therefore, we need 8 cups of sugar if we have 4 cups of water.
