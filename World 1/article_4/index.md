:::title
Decimals
:::

A decimal is a number that uses a decimal point (a dot) to represent values that fall between whole numbers.

Examples: 2.5, 23.45, 63.98

Each digit has a place value. For the basics:

The digit two places to the left of the decimal point is in the **tens** place.  

The digit one place to the left of the decimal point is in the **ones** place.  

The digit one place to the right of the decimal point is in the **tenths** place.  

The digit two places to the right of the decimal point is in the **hundredths** place.  

When adding decimals, add as if they are whole numbers, keeping the decimal points lined up.

:::example
2.54 + 16.57 = 19.11
:::

When subtracting decimals, subtract as if they are whole numbers, keeping the decimal points lined up.

:::example
18.29 − 10.11 = 8.18
:::

When multiplying decimals, multiply as if they are whole numbers. Then count the total number of digits to the right of the decimal point in both numbers. Place the decimal point in the product so it has that many digits to the right.

:::example
9.54 × 10.22

9.54 has two digits to the right of the decimal point.
10.22 has two digits to the right of the decimal point.

2 + 2 = 4

954 × 1022 = 974988

Place the decimal point so there are 4 digits to the right:

97.4988
:::


When dividing decimals, multiply both the dividend and divisor by the same power of 10 until the divisor is a whole number. Then divide.

:::example
12.1 ÷ 0.11

Multiply both numbers by 100:

12.1 × 100 = 1210
0.11 × 100 = 11

1210 ÷ 11 = 110
:::
