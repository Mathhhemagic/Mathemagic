:::title
Fractions
::: 

A **fraction** is a part of a whole.
:::example
½, ⅕, ²⁄₉, ⁴⁄₁₁
:::  

The number on the top of a fraction is the **numerator**.  

The number on the bottom of a fraction is the **denominator**.  

To simplify a fraction, divide the numerator and denominator by their **greatest common factor (GCF)**.  

:::example
⁸⁄₁₂ = ⅔ (Divide both by 4.)
:::


An **improper fraction** has a numerator greater than the denominator.  

:::example
⁷⁄₄
:::  

A **mixed number** combines a whole number and a fraction.  

:::example
1¾
:::  

You can convert between them.

:::example
⁷⁄₄ = 1¾
:::

When adding and subtracting fractions, you add or subtract the numerators and keep the denominator only if the denominators are already the same.
:::example
³⁄₂ + ½ = ⁴⁄₂ = 2
:::  

When adding and subtracting fractions, you must have a **common denominator**.
:::example
⅕ and ⅖ have a common (or the same) denominator.
:::  

You can turn a fraction into an equivalent (equal) fraction by multiplying the numerator and denominator by the same number.
:::example
⅖ = ⁴⁄₁₀ (Multiply the numerator and denominator by 2.)
:::  

So, if we were to solve ⅒ + ³⁄₂₀, we multiply the numerator and denominator of ⅒ by 2, giving us ²⁄₂₀.  

Now we can add:  
:::example
²⁄₂₀ + ³⁄₂₀ = ⁵⁄₂₀ = ¼
:::  

Use the   **least common denominator (LCD)**, which is the smallest denominator both fractions can share.
:::example
With ⅕ and ⅙, the LCD is 30.
:::
You may also need to change both fractions.
:::example
⅕ - ⅙
:::
One denominator cannot be multiplied by a whole number to get the other, so both fractions need to be converted.
:::example
⅕: Multiply the numerator and denominator by 6.  

⅙: Multiply the numerator and denominator by 5.  

⁶⁄₃₀ - ⁵⁄₃₀ = ¹⁄₃₀
:::

When multiplying fractions, simply multiply the numerators together and multiply the denominators together.
:::example
⅕ × ⅝ = ⁵⁄₄₀ = ⅛
:::

When dividing fractions, follow the principle:
:::tip
Keep, Change, Flip.
:::
:::example
⅙ ÷ ²⁄₉
:::
Keep the first fraction, change ÷ into ×, and flip ²⁄₉ into ⁹⁄₂.
:::tip
Note: The flip step changes the fraction into its reciprocal by switching the numerator and denominator.
:::
Now we have:
⅙ × ⁹⁄₂ = ⁹⁄₁₂ = ¾
