:::title

Advanced Geometry

:::

Geometry problems on the SAT can combine several skills at once. You may need to use similarity, transformations, angle relationships, and formulas together to solve a problem.

:::mini-title

Similarity

:::

Two figures are **similar** if they have the same shape but not necessarily the same size.

Similar figures have equal corresponding angles and proportional corresponding side lengths.

:::example

Two similar triangles have corresponding side lengths of 4 and 10.

10⁄4 = 2.5

The scale factor from the smaller triangle to the larger triangle is 2.5.

:::

You can use proportions to find missing side lengths in similar figures.

:::example

Two triangles are similar. A side of the smaller triangle is 6, and the corresponding side of the larger triangle is 15. Another side of the smaller triangle is 8. Find the corresponding side of the larger triangle.

6⁄15 = 8⁄x

6x = 120

x = 20

:::

When two triangles have two pairs of equal corresponding angles, they are similar by **AA similarity**.

:::tip

When working with similar figures, match corresponding sides before setting up a proportion.

:::

:::mini-title

Transformations

:::

A **transformation** changes the position, size, or orientation of a figure.

The main transformations are translations, reflections, rotations, and dilations.

A **translation** moves a figure without changing its size or orientation.

:::example

The point (2, 3) is translated 4 units right and 2 units down.

(2 + 4, 3 − 2)

= (6, 1)

:::

A **reflection** flips a figure across a line.

Reflecting a point across the x-axis changes the sign of its y-coordinate.

:::example

(3, 5) → (3, −5)

:::

Reflecting a point across the y-axis changes the sign of its x-coordinate.

:::example

(3, 5) → (−3, 5)

:::



A **rotation** turns a figure around a fixed point.

A 90° counterclockwise rotation about the origin changes (x, y) to (−y, x).

:::example

(2, 4) → (−4, 2)

:::


A **dilation** changes the size of a figure by a scale factor while keeping its shape the same.

:::example

A point (2, 3) is dilated by a scale factor of 2 about the origin.

(2 × 2, 3 × 2)

= (4, 6)

:::

:::mini-title

Hard Geometry Modeling

:::

Some SAT geometry problems do not give you a formula directly. Instead, you may need to create a diagram or connect several pieces of information.

Start by identifying the shapes in the problem and writing down the information you know.

:::example

A rectangular garden is 12 feet long and 8 feet wide. A path of equal width is built around the outside of the garden. The total area, including the path, is 192 square feet. Find the width of the path.


Let the width of the path be x.

The new length is 12 + 2x, and the new width is 8 + 2x.

Set up the area equation:

(12 + 2x)(8 + 2x) = 192

96 + 40x + 4x² = 192

4x² + 40x − 96 = 0

x² + 10x − 24 = 0

(x + 12)(x − 2) = 0

x = −12 or x = 2

A width cannot be negative, so the path is 2 feet wide.

:::

More difficult geometry problems may require combining similarity with the Pythagorean theorem, area formulas, or coordinate geometry.

:::example

Two similar triangles have a scale factor of 3. The smaller triangle has an area of 12 square units.

The area scale factor is the square of the side-length scale factor.

3² = 9

12 × 9 = 108

The larger triangle has an area of 108 square units.

:::

This is important because areas do not increase by the same factor as side lengths.

:::tip

For difficult geometry problems, break the figure into smaller parts. Look for familiar triangles, rectangles, circles, similar figures, and right triangles before trying to solve the entire problem at once.

:::
