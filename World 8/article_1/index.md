:::title

Trigonometry

:::

**Trigonometry** is the study of the relationships between the angles and side lengths of triangles.

On the SAT, trigonometry is mainly used with right triangles.

:::mini-title

SOH CAH TOA

:::

The three main trigonometric ratios are **sine**, **cosine**, and **tangent**.

SOH CAH TOA helps you remember which sides to use:

sin(θ) = opposite⁄hypotenuse

cos(θ) = adjacent⁄hypotenuse

tan(θ) = opposite⁄adjacent



The **opposite** side is across from the angle. The **adjacent** side is next to the angle, but is not the hypotenuse. The **hypotenuse** is always opposite the right angle.

:::example

A right triangle has an angle of 30°. The side opposite the angle is 5, and the hypotenuse is 10.

sin(30°) = 5⁄10

sin(30°) = 1⁄2

:::

:::tip

Identify the angle you are working with first. The opposite and adjacent sides depend on which angle you choose.

:::

:::mini-title

Finding Missing Sides

:::

Trigonometric ratios can be used to find a missing side when you know an angle and another side.

Choose the ratio that uses the sides you know and the side you want to find.

:::example

A right triangle has an angle of 40° and a hypotenuse of 12. Find the side opposite the 40° angle.

sin(40°) = x⁄12

12sin(40°) = x

x ≈ 7.7

:::

If the missing side is adjacent to the angle and the hypotenuse is known, use cosine.

:::example

A right triangle has an angle of 60° and a hypotenuse of 10. Find the adjacent side.

cos(60°) = x⁄10

10cos(60°) = x

x = 5

:::

If you know the opposite and adjacent sides, use tangent.

:::example

A right triangle has an angle of 35° and an adjacent side of 8. Find the opposite side.

tan(35°) = x⁄8

8tan(35°) = x

x ≈ 5.6

:::

:::tip

Make sure your calculator is in **degree mode** when working with angles measured in degrees.

:::

:::mini-title

Finding Missing Angles

:::

You can use inverse trigonometric functions to find a missing angle.

The three inverse functions are sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, and tan⁻¹.

:::example

A right triangle has an opposite side of 6 and a hypotenuse of 10.

sin(θ) = 6⁄10

θ = sin⁻¹(0.6)

θ ≈ 36.9°

:::




:::example

A right triangle has an opposite side of 5 and an adjacent side of 12.

tan(θ) = 5⁄12

θ = tan⁻¹(5⁄12)

θ ≈ 22.6°

:::

:::mini-title

Angle of Elevation

:::

The **angle of elevation** is the angle measured upward from a horizontal line to an object.

These problems can often be modeled using a right triangle.

:::example

A person stands 50 feet from the base of a building. The angle of elevation to the top of the building is 40°. Find the height of the building.

tan(40°) = h⁄50

50tan(40°) = h

h ≈ 42.0

The building is approximately 42 feet tall.

:::

The same idea can be used for angles of depression. An angle of depression is measured downward from a horizontal line.

:::tip

For angle of elevation problems, draw a right triangle and identify the angle, the opposite side, and the adjacent side before choosing a trigonometric ratio.

:::
