:::title

Circles

:::

A **circle** is a set of points that are all the same distance from a fixed point called the center.

The distance from the center to any point on the circle is the **radius**. The distance across the circle through the center is the **diameter**.

:::mini-title

Arc Length

:::

An **arc** is a portion of the circumference of a circle. The length of an arc depends on the radius of the circle and the angle of the arc.

For an angle measured in degrees, the arc length is:

Arc length = θ⁄360 × 2πr


::contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}


:::example

A circle has a radius of 6 and a central angle of 90°.

Arc length = 90⁄360 × 2π(6)

Arc length = 1⁄4 × 12π

Arc length = 3π

:::

A central angle measures the angle formed by two radii of the circle.

:::example

A circle has a radius of 10 and a central angle of 72°.

Arc length = 72⁄360 × 2π(10)

Arc length = 1⁄5 × 20π

Arc length = 4π

:::

:::tip

The fraction θ⁄360 tells you what fraction of the entire circle the arc represents.

:::

:::mini-title

Sectors

:::

A **sector** is a region of a circle enclosed by two radii and an arc.

The area of a sector is the same fraction of the circle's total area as its central angle is of 360°.

Sector area = θ⁄360 × πr²


::contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}


:::example

A circle has a radius of 8 and a central angle of 90°.

Sector area = 90⁄360 × π(8)²

Sector area = 1⁄4 × 64π

Sector area = 16π

:::

A semicircle is a sector with a central angle of 180°. It has half the area of the entire circle.

:::example

A circle has a radius of 5.

Semicircle area = 180⁄360 × π(5)²

Semicircle area = 25π⁄2

:::

:::mini-title

Radians Basics

:::

A **radian** is another way to measure angles.

A full circle measures 2π radians, while a half circle measures π radians.

Some common angle conversions are:

180° = π radians

90° = π⁄2 radians

60° = π⁄3 radians

45° = π⁄4 radians

30° = π⁄6 radians

:::example

Convert 60° to radians.

60 × π⁄180

= π⁄3

:::

To convert radians to degrees, multiply by 180⁄π.

:::example

Convert π⁄4 radians to degrees.

π⁄4 × 180⁄π

= 45°

:::

When an angle is measured in radians, the arc length formula becomes:

s = rθ

where s is the arc length, r is the radius, and θ is the angle in radians.

:::example

A circle has a radius of 6 and a central angle of π⁄3 radians.

s = 6 × π⁄3

s = 2π

:::

:::tip

When using s = rθ, make sure the angle is in radians, not degrees.

:::
