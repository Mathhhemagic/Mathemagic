:::title
SAT Word Problems
:::

Many SAT questions describe a real-world situation in words instead of giving you an equation directly. The key skill is translating the words into math.

:::mini-title
Rates
:::

Rate problems usually involve distance, speed, and time, connected by the formula:

distance = rate × time

:::example
A train travels at 60 miles per hour for 3 hours. How far does it travel?  

distance = 60 × 3 = 180 miles
:::

:::example
A car travels 240 miles in 4 hours. What is its rate?  

rate = 240 ÷ 4 = 60 miles per hour
:::

:::tip
Make sure your units match before plugging into the formula (e.g., convert minutes to hours if the rate is given per hour).
:::

:::mini-title
Work Problems
:::

Work problems describe how long it takes one or more people or machines to complete a task, usually using rates of work per unit of time.

:::example
A machine can complete a job in 6 hours. What fraction of the job does it complete in 1 hour?  

Rate = ⅙ of the job per hour
:::

:::example
Machine A completes a job in 4 hours, and Machine B completes it in 6 hours. Working together, how long does it take?

Combined rate = ¼ + ⅙ = ³⁄₁₂ + ²⁄₁₂ = ⁵⁄₁₂ of the job per hour

Time = 1 ÷ ⁵⁄₁₂ = ¹²⁄₅ = 2.4 hours
:::

:::mini-title
Cost/Value Problems
:::

Cost problems involve a fixed cost, a variable cost, or a total value made up of different items at different prices.

:::example
A parking garage charges $5 plus $2 per hour. What is the cost for 3 hours?  

cost = 5 + 2(3) = 5 + 6 = 11
:::

:::example
A movie theater sells adult tickets for $12 and child tickets for $8. If 5 adult tickets and 3 child tickets are sold, what is the total revenue?  

revenue = 12(5) + 8(3) = 60 + 24 = 84
:::

:::mini-title
Mixtures
:::

Mixture problems involve combining two quantities with different values (like concentrations or prices) to form a new mixture.

:::example
How many liters of a 20% acid solution must be mixed with a 50% acid solution to make 30 liters of a 30% solution?

Let x = liters of the 20% solution, so (30 − x) = liters of the 50% solution.

0.20x + 0.50(30 − x) = 0.30(30)  

0.20x + 15 − 0.50x = 9  

−0.30x = −6  

x = 20

So 20 liters of the 20% solution and 10 liters of the 50% solution are needed.
:::

:::tip
Set up mixture problems by multiplying each amount by its percent or price, then set the sum equal to the total mixture's percent or price.
:::

:::mini-title
Consecutive Integers
:::

Consecutive integer problems ask you to find integers that follow one another in order.

For consecutive integers, use: x, x + 1, x + 2, ...

For consecutive even or odd integers, use: x, x + 2, x + 4, ...

:::example
The sum of three consecutive integers is 96. Find the integers.

x + (x + 1) + (x + 2) = 96  

3x + 3 = 96  

3x = 93  

x = 31

The integers are 31, 32, and 33.
:::

:::example
The sum of two consecutive even integers is 42. Find the integers.

x + (x + 2) = 42  

2x + 2 = 42  

2x = 40  

x = 20

The integers are 20 and 22.
:::

:::tip
Always define your variables clearly at the start of a word problem before writing the equation.
:::
