:::title
Modeling
:::

Modeling means turning a real-world description into a mathematical expression or equation. This is one of the most heavily tested skills on the Digital SAT.

:::mini-title
Creating Equations from Words
:::

To build an equation, identify what is known, what is unknown, and how the quantities relate to each other.

:::example
A phone plan costs $20 per month plus $0.10 per text message.  

Let x = number of text messages, and y = total monthly cost.  

y = 0.10x + 20
:::

:::example
A number increased by 8 is equal to twice the number.  

Let x = the number.  

x + 8 = 2x
:::

:::tip
Look for a fixed amount (stays the same) and a variable amount (changes based on something) when setting up an equation.
:::

:::mini-title
Translating Expressions
:::

Certain words consistently correspond to specific operations.

**Sum**, **more than**, **increased by**, and **total** mean addition.

**Difference**, **less than**, and **decreased by** mean subtraction.

**Product**, **times**, and **of** mean multiplication.

**Quotient** and **per** mean division.

:::example
"5 less than a number" translates to x − 5, not 5 − x.
:::

:::example
"The product of a number and 7" translates to 7x.
:::

:::tip
Be careful with "less than" and "subtracted from"—these reverse the order of the subtraction compared to how the sentence reads.
:::

:::mini-title
Variables in Context
:::

On the SAT, variables and expressions often represent real quantities, not just abstract letters. Understanding what a variable or expression represents is key to answering questions correctly, especially when asked to interpret an equation rather than solve it.

:::example
In the equation y = 15x + 40, where y is total cost and x is number of hours:  

15 represents the cost per hour.  

40 represents a fixed starting cost, such as a flat fee.
:::

:::example
In the equation P = 200 − 4t, where P is a plant's height in centimeters and t is time in weeks:  

−4 represents the rate at which the plant's height decreases each week.  

200 represents the plant's height at t = 0, the starting height.
:::

:::tip
When asked what a number or variable "represents," connect it back to the real-world quantities described in the problem, not just its position in the equation.
:::
