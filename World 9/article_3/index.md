:::title

Scatterplots

:::

A **scatterplot** is a graph that shows the relationship between two variables. Each point represents one pair of values.

:::mini-title

Correlation

:::

**Correlation** describes the direction and strength of a relationship between two variables.

A positive correlation means that as one variable increases, the other tends to increase.

:::example

A scatterplot shows that students who spend more time studying tend to have higher test scores.

:::

This represents a positive correlation.

A negative correlation means that as one variable increases, the other tends to decrease.

:::example

A scatterplot shows that as the amount of time a car spends traveling at a certain speed increases, the amount of fuel remaining tends to decrease.

:::

A scatterplot can also have little or no correlation if the points do not follow a clear pattern.

:::tip

Correlation describes a relationship between variables. It does not necessarily mean that one variable causes the other.

:::

:::mini-title

Line of Best Fit

:::

A **line of best fit** is a line that represents the overall trend of a scatterplot.

The line does not need to pass through every point. Instead, it should follow the general pattern of the data.

A line of best fit can be used to make predictions.

:::example

A line of best fit models the relationship between hours studied, x, and test score, y.

y = 8x + 60

:::

If a student studies for 5 hours, substitute x = 5 to estimate the test score.

:::example

y = 8(5) + 60

y = 100

:::

The predicted test score is 100.

:::mini-title

Trends

:::

When interpreting a scatterplot, look for the overall **trend** of the points.

A trend can be positive, negative, or have little to no association.

The closer the points are to a line, the stronger the linear relationship tends to be.

:::example

If the points in a scatterplot generally rise from left to right, the data has a positive linear trend.

:::

If the points generally fall from left to right, the data has a negative linear trend.

:::example

A scatterplot shows points that generally fall from left to right.

This represents a negative linear trend.

:::

On the SAT, you may be asked to interpret a scatterplot, identify its trend, or use a line of best fit to make a prediction.

:::tip

When making a prediction from a scatterplot, use the line of best fit rather than choosing the point that happens to be closest to the given x-value.

:::
