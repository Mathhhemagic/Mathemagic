:::title

Probability

:::

**Probability** describes how likely an event is to happen.

A probability can be written as a fraction, decimal, or percent. It is always between 0 and 1, or between 0% and 100%.

:::mini-title

Basic Probability

:::

For equally likely outcomes, probability is the number of favorable outcomes divided by the total number of possible outcomes.

P(event) = favorable outcomes⁄total outcomes


:::example

A fair six-sided die is rolled. What is the probability of rolling a 4?

There is 1 favorable outcome and 6 possible outcomes.

P(4) = 1⁄6

:::

The probability of an event that cannot happen is 0. The probability of an event that must happen is 1.

:::example

What is the probability of rolling a number from 1 to 6 on a standard six-sided die?

P = 6⁄6

P = 1

:::

The probability of an event not happening is called its **complement**.

:::example

The probability of rain tomorrow is 30%.

The probability that it does not rain is:

100% − 30% = 70%

:::

:::tip

If you know the probability of an event, subtract it from 1 to find the probability that the event does not happen.

:::

:::mini-title

Independent Events

:::

Two events are **independent** if the outcome of one event does not affect the probability of the other event.

For independent events, the probability that both events happen is found by multiplying their probabilities.




:::example

A coin is flipped twice. What is the probability of getting heads both times?

P(heads) = 1⁄2

P(heads and heads) = 1⁄2 × 1⁄2

P(heads and heads) = 1⁄4

:::

Rolling a die and flipping a coin are also independent events.

:::example

What is the probability of rolling a 6 and flipping heads?

P(6) = 1⁄6

P(heads) = 1⁄2

P(6 and heads) = 1⁄6 × 1⁄2

P(6 and heads) = 1⁄12

:::

:::mini-title

Compound Probability

:::

A **compound probability** involves two or more events.

When finding the probability that two independent events both happen, multiply their probabilities.

:::example

A bag contains 3 red marbles and 2 blue marbles. A marble is chosen, replaced, and then another marble is chosen. What is the probability of choosing red both times?

P(red) = 3⁄5

Because the first marble is replaced, the events are independent.

P(red and red) = 3⁄5 × 3⁄5

P(red and red) = 9⁄25

:::

When finding the probability that either of two mutually exclusive events happens, add their probabilities.

:::example

A fair six-sided die is rolled. What is the probability of rolling a 2 or a 5?

P(2) = 1⁄6

P(5) = 1⁄6

P(2 or 5) = 1⁄6 + 1⁄6

P(2 or 5) = 1⁄3

:::

The events are mutually exclusive because a single roll cannot be both 2 and 5.

:::tip

For "and," think about multiplying probabilities. For mutually exclusive "or" events, add the probabilities.

:::
