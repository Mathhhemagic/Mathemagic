:::title

Coordinate Geometry

:::

**Coordinate geometry** uses algebra to describe points, lines, and shapes on the coordinate plane.

The coordinate plane has an x-axis and a y-axis. A point is written as (x, y), where x tells you the horizontal position and y tells you the vertical position.

:::mini-title

Distance Formula

:::

The **distance formula** can be used to find the distance between two points.

For points (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂), the formula is:

d = √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²)

The formula comes from the Pythagorean theorem. The differences in the x-values and y-values form the legs of a right triangle.

:::example

Find the distance between (2, 3) and (6, 6).

d = √((6 − 2)² + (6 − 3)²)

d = √(4² + 3²)

d = √25

d = 5

:::

:::tip

You can subtract the coordinates in either order because the differences are squared.

:::

:::mini-title

Midpoint Formula

:::

The **midpoint** of a line segment is the point exactly halfway between its two endpoints.

To find the midpoint, average the x-coordinates and average the y-coordinates.

For points (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂):

Midpoint = ((x₁ + x₂)⁄2, (y₁ + y₂)⁄2)

:::example

Find the midpoint of (2, 4) and (8, 10).

x-coordinate:

(2 + 8)⁄2 = 5

y-coordinate:

(4 + 10)⁄2 = 7

Midpoint = (5, 7)

:::

The midpoint formula can also be used when one endpoint or the midpoint is unknown.

:::example

One endpoint of a segment is (2, 5), and its midpoint is (6, 8). Find the other endpoint.

For the x-coordinate:

(2 + x)⁄2 = 6

2 + x = 12

x = 10

For the y-coordinate:

(5 + y)⁄2 = 8

5 + y = 16

y = 11

The other endpoint is (10, 11).

:::

:::mini-title

Circle Equations

:::

The standard form of a circle equation is:

(x − h)² + (y − k)² = r²

The center of the circle is (h, k), and r is the radius.

:::example

(x − 3)² + (y + 2)² = 25

:::

The center is (3, −2), and the radius is 5.

Be careful with the signs. The signs inside the parentheses are opposite the coordinates of the center.

:::example

(x + 4)² + (y − 1)² = 16

:::

The center is (−4, 1), and the radius is 4.

:::tip

In the standard circle equation, the number inside each parenthesis has the opposite sign of the corresponding coordinate of the center.

:::

A circle centered at the origin has an especially simple equation:

x² + y² = r²

:::example

x² + y² = 49

:::

The center is (0, 0), and the radius is 7.
