:::title

Volume

:::

**Volume** is the amount of space inside a three-dimensional figure.

Volume is measured in cubic units.

:::mini-title

Prisms

:::

A **prism** is a three-dimensional figure with two matching, parallel bases.

The volume of a prism is:

V = Bh

where B is the area of the base and h is the height of the prism.

For a rectangular prism, the formula can be written as:

V = lwh


::contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}


:::example

A rectangular prism has a length of 8 inches, a width of 3 inches, and a height of 5 inches.

V = 8(3)(5)

V = 120 cubic inches

:::

For a prism with a triangular base, first find the area of the triangle, then multiply by the height of the prism.

:::example

A triangular prism has a triangular base with a base of 6 centimeters and a height of 4 centimeters. The prism has a length of 10 centimeters.

B = 6(4)⁄2

B = 12

V = 12(10)

V = 120 cubic centimeters

:::

:::mini-title

Cylinders

:::

A **cylinder** has two circular bases.

The volume of a cylinder is:

V = πr²h

where r is the radius of the base and h is the height.


::contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}


:::example

A cylinder has a radius of 3 meters and a height of 8 meters.

V = π(3)²(8)

V = 72π cubic meters

:::

If the diameter is given, divide it by 2 to find the radius before using the formula.

:::example

A cylinder has a diameter of 10 inches and a height of 6 inches.

r = 10⁄2

r = 5

V = π(5)²(6)

V = 150π cubic inches

:::

:::tip

The radius, not the diameter, is used in the cylinder volume formula.

:::

:::mini-title

Spheres

:::

A **sphere** is a three-dimensional figure in which every point on the surface is the same distance from the center.

The volume of a sphere is:

V = 4πr³⁄3

where r is the radius.


::contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}


:::example

A sphere has a radius of 3 feet.

V = 4π(3)³⁄3

V = 36π cubic feet

:::

If the diameter is given, divide it by 2 before finding the volume.

:::example

A sphere has a diameter of 8 centimeters.

r = 8⁄2

r = 4

V = 4π(4)³⁄3

V = 256π⁄3 cubic centimeters

:::

:::tip

For volume problems, make sure all measurements use the same units before calculating.

:::
