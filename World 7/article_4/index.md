:::title

Pythagorean Theorem

:::

The **Pythagorean theorem** can be used to find a missing side of a right triangle.

The theorem states:

a² + b² = c²

where a and b are the legs of the right triangle and c is the **hypotenuse**.




:::mini-title

Finding a Missing Side

:::

If the hypotenuse is missing, substitute the two known legs into the formula and solve for c.

:::example

A right triangle has legs of 6 and 8.

6² + 8² = c²

36 + 64 = c²

100 = c²

c = 10

:::

If a leg is missing, subtract the known leg from c² before taking the square root.

:::example

A right triangle has a hypotenuse of 13 and a leg of 5.

5² + b² = 13²

25 + b² = 169

b² = 144

b = 12

:::

:::tip

The hypotenuse is always opposite the right angle and is always the longest side.

:::

:::mini-title

Distance Applications

:::

The Pythagorean theorem can be used to find distances in real-world situations.

For example, if you know the horizontal and vertical distances between two points, they form the legs of a right triangle.

:::example

A person walks 3 miles east and then 4 miles north. How far is the person from the starting point?

3² + 4² = c²

9 + 16 = c²

25 = c²

c = 5

The person is 5 miles from the starting point.

:::

The same idea can be used on a coordinate plane. The horizontal and vertical changes between two points form the legs of a right triangle.



:::example

Find the distance between (1, 2) and (4, 6).

The horizontal distance is:

4 − 1 = 3

The vertical distance is:

6 − 2 = 4

3² + 4² = d²

9 + 16 = d²

d = 5

:::

:::mini-title

SAT Geometry Shortcuts

:::

Some right triangles use common Pythagorean triples. These are sets of whole numbers that satisfy the Pythagorean theorem.

The most common one is:

3, 4, 5

Other useful triples include:

5, 12, 13

8, 15, 17

7, 24, 25

:::example

A right triangle has legs of 9 and 12.

This is a multiple of the 3-4-5 triangle.

3 × 3 = 9

4 × 3 = 12

5 × 3 = 15

The hypotenuse is 15.

:::

Recognizing these patterns can save time on the SAT.

:::tip

If you see side lengths that are multiples of 3-4-5, 5-12-13, or another Pythagorean triple, you may be able to find the missing side without doing the full calculation.

:::
