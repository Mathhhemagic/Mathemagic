:::title

Triangles

:::

A **triangle** is a polygon with three sides and three angles. The angles and side lengths of a triangle are related by several important rules.

:::mini-title

Similar Triangles

:::

**Similar triangles** have the same shape, but they may have different sizes. Their corresponding angles are equal, and their corresponding side lengths are proportional.

:::example

Triangle A has side lengths 3, 4, and 5.

Triangle B has side lengths 6, 8, and 10.

6⁄3 = 8⁄4 = 10⁄5 = 2

:::

The triangles are similar because their corresponding side lengths have the same ratio.

A scale factor tells you how much larger or smaller one figure is than another.

:::example

Two similar triangles have corresponding sides of 5 and 15.

15⁄5 = 3

The scale factor is 3.

:::

:::tip

Match corresponding sides carefully. The order of the vertices in similar triangles can help identify which sides correspond.

:::

:::mini-title

Triangle Rules

:::

The three interior angles of a triangle always add to 180°.

:::example

A triangle has angles of 45° and 65°.

x = 180° − 45° − 65°

x = 70°

:::

The **exterior angle theorem** states that an exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles.

:::example

A triangle has two opposite interior angles of 50° and 70°.

x = 50° + 70°

x = 120°

:::

The side lengths of a triangle must satisfy the **triangle inequality**. The sum of any two sides must be greater than the third side.

:::example

Can sides of lengths 4, 7, and 10 form a triangle?

4 + 7 > 10

11 > 10

7 + 10 > 4

17 > 4

4 + 10 > 7

14 > 7

:::

Since all three conditions are true, the side lengths can form a triangle.

:::tip

To check whether three side lengths can form a triangle, make sure the sum of every pair of sides is greater than the remaining side.

:::

:::mini-title

Right Triangles

:::

A **right triangle** has one angle measuring 90°.

The side opposite the right angle is called the **hypotenuse**. It is always the longest side of a right triangle.

The **Pythagorean theorem** relates the three side lengths of a right triangle:

a² + b² = c²

where c is the hypotenuse.

:::example

A right triangle has legs of 6 and 8.

6² + 8² = c²

36 + 64 = c²

100 = c²

c = 10

:::

:::mini-title

Special Right Triangles

:::

Two special right triangles appear frequently on the SAT: 45°-45°-90° triangles and 30°-60°-90° triangles.

A **45°-45°-90° triangle** has two equal legs. If each leg has length x, the hypotenuse has length x√2.

:::example

A 45°-45°-90° triangle has a leg of length 5.

Hypotenuse = 5√2

:::

A **30°-60°-90° triangle** has side lengths in the ratio 1 : √3 : 2.

The side opposite 30° is the shortest side. If the shortest side has length x, the longer leg has length x√3 and the hypotenuse has length 2x.

:::example

A 30°-60°-90° triangle has a shortest side of length 4.

Longer leg = 4√3

Hypotenuse = 8

:::

:::tip

For a 45°-45°-90° triangle, the hypotenuse is the leg multiplied by √2.

For a 30°-60°-90° triangle, the sides are x, x√3, and 2x.

:::
