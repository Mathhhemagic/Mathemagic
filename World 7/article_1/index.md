:::title

Area & Perimeter

:::

**Perimeter** is the distance around a figure. **Area** is the amount of space inside a figure.

Perimeter is measured in units, while area is measured in square units.

:::mini-title

Rectangles

:::

The perimeter of a rectangle is found by adding all four sides. Since opposite sides have equal lengths, the formula is:

P = 2l + 2w

The area of a rectangle is:

A = lw

:::example

A rectangle has a length of 8 inches and a width of 5 inches.

P = 2(8) + 2(5)

P = 26 inches

A = 8(5)

A = 40 square inches

:::

A square is a rectangle with four equal sides. Its area can be found using:

A = s²

:::example

A square has a side length of 6 feet.

A = 6²

A = 36 square feet

:::

:::mini-title

Triangles

:::

The area of a triangle is half the area of a rectangle with the same base and height.

A = bh⁄2

The height must be perpendicular to the base.

:::example

A triangle has a base of 10 centimeters and a height of 6 centimeters.

A = 10(6)⁄2

A = 30 square centimeters

:::

The perimeter of a triangle is the sum of its three side lengths.

:::example

A triangle has side lengths of 7, 8, and 10.

P = 7 + 8 + 10

P = 25

:::

:::mini-title

Circles

:::

The **circumference** is the distance around a circle.

C = 2πr

The area of a circle is:

A = πr²

The radius is the distance from the center of the circle to its edge. The diameter is twice the radius.

:::example

A circle has a radius of 4.

C = 2π(4)

C = 8π

A = π(4)²

A = 16π

:::

If the diameter is given instead of the radius, divide the diameter by 2 to find the radius.

:::example

A circle has a diameter of 12.

r = 12⁄2

r = 6

A = π(6)²

A = 36π

:::

:::tip

Make sure you use the radius, not the diameter, when using A = πr².

:::

:::mini-title

Composite Figures

:::

A **composite figure** is a shape made from two or more simpler figures.

To find the area of a composite figure, divide it into familiar shapes. Find the area of each shape and then add or subtract as needed.

:::example

A figure is made from a rectangle measuring 10 by 6 and a rectangle measuring 4 by 3.

Area of the first rectangle:

A = 10(6)

A = 60

Area of the second rectangle:

A = 4(3)

A = 12

Total area:

60 + 12 = 72 square units

:::

Sometimes it is easier to find the area of a large shape and subtract the missing part.

:::example

A large rectangle has an area of 80 square units. A 3 by 4 rectangle is removed from one corner.

Area of removed rectangle:

A = 3(4)

A = 12

Remaining area:

80 − 12 = 68 square units

:::

:::tip

For composite figures, look for rectangles, triangles, and circles that you already know how to find the area of. Then add or subtract their areas.
:::
